About Me

Dr. Xiaole Yin is a dedicated and accomplished researcher in the field of bioinformatics, with a strong focus on the human microbiome, environmental microbiome, antibiotic resistance, and multi-omics. Currently, Dr. Yin holds the position of Research Fellow in Medicine at Brigham and Women's Hospital and Harvard Medical School, a role she has occupied since her postdoctoral appointment began.

Her academic journey commenced with a Bachelor's Degree from Shandong University. Following this, she pursued and obtained her MPhil degree. Dr. Yin's doctoral studies were undertaken at The University of Hong Kong, where she earned her Ph.D. in 2021 under the supervision of Ir. Prof. Tong Zhang. Her doctoral research centered on the development of bioinformatic tools and the exploration of antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) and mobile genetic elements (MGEs) in various environmental contexts. This work involved significant contributions to the ARGs-OAP (Antibiotic Resistance Genes - Online Analysis Pipeline), including the development of ARGs-OAP 2.0 with an expanded SARG (Structured Antibiotic Resistance Genes) database and a novel module based on Hidden Markov Models to enhance the detection and discovery of ARGs. Further updates led to ARGs-OAP 3.0, which incorporated visualization packages for more efficient interpretation of ARG profiles and a continuously updated SARG 3.0 database.

During her Ph.D., Dr. Yin conducted extensive research, including a nine-year longitudinal metagenomic study of a wastewater treatment plant to characterize the temporal variation of its resistome profile. Her findings demonstrated significant changes in both the abundance and structure of the resistome over time, implying successive selection pressures within the activated sludge system. She also performed comprehensive risk assessments of ARGs in wastewater treatment plants in Hong Kong, comparing their prevalence and MGEs with data from North America, South America, Europe, and Asia. Her research highlighted the high ARG removal efficiency of Hong Kong's WWTPs. Furthermore, Dr. Yin's work involved a broad-scale comparison of ARG pollution levels across diverse ecosystems by analyzing hundreds of publicly available metagenomes, revealing uneven proliferation of ARGs and utilizing machine learning tools to decipher distinctions and connections across these ecosystems.

A significant part of her doctoral work also focused on identifying the Horizontal Gene Transfer (HGT) mechanisms of ARGs on integrative and conjugative elements (ICEs) across various taxonomy lineages. This involved analyzing nearly 200,000 genomes and led to the development of ICEcream, a tool designed to classify ICEs and ARGs simultaneously from bacterial genomes. This research identified important ICE families and ARG types carried by them, highlighting the underestimated role of ICEs in ARG dissemination and evolution.

Before her current postdoctoral position at Harvard Medical School, Dr. Yin also held a Postdoc position at Hong Kong University, which concluded in December 2023. She has been an international visiting student at the Scientific Research National Center (CNRS-ISTO) in Orleans, France, under the advisement of Prof. Fatima Laggoun in 2015.

Research Interests

My research focuses on bioinformatics, particularly in the areas of genomics, computational biology, and the application of machine learning techniques to biological data. I am passionate about understanding the complexities of the human microbiome, investigating the mechanisms of antibiotic resistance in environmental settings, and developing novel multi-omics approaches to unravel biological systems. My work aims to contribute to the development of new diagnostic and therapeutic strategies by leveraging large-scale biological datasets and advanced computational methodologies. I am currently seeking highly motivated PhD students to join my group and explore these exciting research frontiers.
